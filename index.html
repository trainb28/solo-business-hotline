First, what we're actually talking about

A solo business is not a startup.

It's how you fund the life you want. That's the whole job. Enough income, from work you control, in the fewest hours it takes to get there.

No employees. No investors. No five year plan. No exit.

And it has nothing to do with staying small. Plenty of solo businesses clear seven figures with nobody on payroll. Size was never the constraint. Headcount and hours are.

The trap is hours, not size

Plenty of people build something that pays well and eats every hour they have. That's not a win. That's rebuilding the job you left, except now you chase the invoices too.

Hours are the actual scoreboard

Every hour you don't have to work is an hour that belongs to you. Almost nobody keeps score that way, which is why so many people get out and end up just as buried.

The point is what it buys

Doing what you want, when you want, with who you want. Twenty five years in, you've earned that. The business is just the mechanism that pays for it.

Which is why friction is the enemy. Every week you burn guessing at something another solo could have answered in ten minutes is a week you don't get back. That's the whole reason this exists.

Fair questions

Ask away.

Is there a size you have to be?

No, in either direction. Members range from first client to seven figures, all with nobody on payroll. The line isn't revenue, it's shape: no team to manage, no investors, no exit you're building toward. If that's you, you're in the right place.

Unlimited calls really means unlimited?

Book as often as you need. No credits, no per-call fee, no monthly cap. Most members do a couple a month and then go quiet for a while. Both are fine.

How fast can I get on a call?

Usually within the week, often within a day or two. You'll see open times and book the one that works. Written questions get answered inside 24 hours.

Is this another Slack group I'll mute in a week?

That's the right thing to worry about. The difference is the peer groups and chapters, which are small, scheduled, and the same people every time. You end up knowing eight people well instead of skimming four hundred.

Why the hard stop at thirty minutes?

Because it makes both of you get to the point, and because it's what lets mentors say yes to a lot of calls. An hour would mean fewer mentors and longer waits. Thirty is the trade that works.

Do I have to show up to anything?

No. Some people are in here every day and some surface twice a year with one question. Nobody's tracking your attendance and nobody's going to guilt you about it.

I'm just getting started. Do I belong here?

Yes. Members range from first client to well past six figures, and some still have a job while they build. Nobody here is going to make you feel behind.

What if I want to keep working with a mentor?

Hire them. Directly, on your own terms, no cut to us and no permission needed. Some of the best outcomes here start as a thirty-minute call and turn into something else entirely.

Stop losing weeks to things somebody already solved.

Thirty minutes with the right person beats three weeks of guessing, and you don't have to do the rest of it alone either. Come for the answers. Stay for the people who get it.
